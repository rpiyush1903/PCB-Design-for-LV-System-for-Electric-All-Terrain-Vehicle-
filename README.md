# Team AVEON Racing — e-ATV Vehicle Control Unit (VCU)

## What this is

This is the Vehicle Control Unit (VCU) built by Team AVEON Racing for our electric All-Terrain Vehicle, engineered specifically for the demands of e-BAJA SAE competition . The control unit should be resistant to high vibration, rough terrain, electrical noise etc.

The VCU sits at the center of the car's low-voltage control system. It reads every driver input ,ignition Key, cockpit and chassis kill switches, brake pressure switch, accelerator position sensor, and Forward/Neutral/Reverse selection(Rotary Switch) and turns that into safe, Low Voltage control over the high-voltage tractive system. It continuously monitors the safety loop, runs the basic starting and running logic of the vehicle , Controls all the Low Voltage and High Voltage components in the vehicle and is built to shut the whole tractive system down immediately if anything looks wrong.


## Hardware Design & PCB Architecture
The whole electrical layout is built with the idea of Simple , Efficient Control and Robust Safety 

The VCU PCB is made to protect the ATmega328P microcontroller from high-voltage transients, high-current switching noise, and off-road mechanical vibration through strategic domain isolation and multi-stage output driving.

## 1. Power Distribution & Domain Isolation

### 48V Tractive Domain
Powers the motor controller through a 160A main fuse, HV cut-off switch, and main Accumulator Isolation Relays (AIRs).

### 12V Accessory Domain
Stepped down via a 48V-to-12V DC-DC converter to power external safety loops, lights, RTDS buzzer, and relay coils.

### 5V Logic Domain
Generated by a dedicated 12V-to-5V buck converter with electrolytic (100 µF) and ceramic (0.1 µF) decoupling capacitors, providing clean power exclusively to the MCU and sensor pull-up networks.

## 2. Optocoupler & Active-LOW Output Drive

### Active-LOW Logic
GPIO output pins require an explicit 0V (LOW) signal to energize outputs. Floating pin states during bootloader execution or CPU resets cannot accidentally trigger relays.

### Galvanic Isolation
Output pins drive PC817 optocouplers, creating an optical barrier between the 5V MCU logic ground and the 12V relay ground plane.

### Low-Side Switching
Optocoupler phototransistors trigger low-side N-Channel MOSFETs/transistors to sink 12V relay coil current, keeping high current draw entirely off the 5V logic rail.

### Flyback Diode Clamping
1N4007 flyback diodes are placed in reversed manner and parallel across all relay coils to safely dissipate back-EMF inductive voltage spikes ($V = -L \frac{di}{dt}$) during de-energization, preventing CMOS latch-up.

## 3. Sensor Input Filtering

### Throttle Conditioning
The Hall-effect accelerator signal (A2) passes through a hardware RC low-pass filter ($R = 1\text{ k}\Omega$, $C = 100\text{ nF}$, $f_c \approx 1.59\text{ kHz}$) to eliminate high-frequency inverter noise generated by the motor controller.

### Digital Inputs
Brake pressure switch (A3), FNR selectors (A4/A5), and safety interlocks (A1) utilize pull-down resistor networks and hardware debouncing to guarantee stable state detection across rough terrain.

### Power Distribution

* **High Voltage Rail:** The 48V accumulator feeds through a 160A main fuse and an HV cut-off switch straight to the motor controller.
* **Low Voltage Rail:** A 48V→12V DC-DC converter powers the vehicle's 12V accessories and the dashboard safety loop.
* **Logic Power:** The ATmega328P and its supporting logic get their own dedicated 12V→5V buck converter, kept isolated from everything else on the low-voltage side.

### Core Components

* **MCU:** ATmega328P
* **Sensor Inputs:** Ignition key, cockpit and chassis kill switches, brake pressure switch, accelerator (Hall-effect sensor), and the Forward/Neutral/Reverse selector all fed from the dashboard cluster.
* **Actuator Outputs:** Relay-driven outputs for the TSAL, reverse light, main contactor (accumulator isolation relay), brake light, and the RTDS buzzer.

## Firmware Architecture

The firmware (C++, compiled through Arduino) is built with the starting sequence in mind and safety as a priority .
